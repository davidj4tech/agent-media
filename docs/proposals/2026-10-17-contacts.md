## What there is

- **Rooms, not people.** Messages lists Matrix rooms (§6.1 "Matrix rows"):
  one per SMS conversation through the Google Messages bridge, one per Signal
  chat, and so on. The same person on SMS and Signal is two rows. A room's
  title is the bridge's name for it: the phone's contact name, else a number.
- **Google Contacts** are the phone's contacts (davidj4@gmail.com). Nothing on
  red5 could read them before step 1. On 24 Sep 2026, adding Lionel's Donvale
  address needed adb, and adb fails off Wi-Fi.
- **The phone's contacts provider** holds them all, read as shell uid with
  `content query` (17 Oct 2026: 2731 contacts; raw contacts 4757
  `com.google`, 469 `com.whatsapp`, merged by Android into one person each).
- **The Drupal CRM** runs on red4 (`drupal-crm`, `~/stacks/drupal-crm`),
  bound to `127.0.0.1:8080` there. It uses Drupal's `crm` module (contact
  entities with `name`, `address` and `telephone` fields), with `jsonapi`,
  `simple_oauth` and `basic_auth` on. It held 4 contacts in September.

## The shape

The app stays generic: the server knows the sources and answers with
contacts, and the app renders what it is given. Most of the work is a new
server module, `contacts.py`, with one adapter per source.

**1. A contact store on the server.** `contacts.py` keeps a merged copy of
both sources in a SQLite file in the state dir, refreshed every few minutes
and on demand. Each contact holds its names, phones (normalised to E.164,
default region AU), emails, addresses, notes, and a `sources` list such as
`[{kind: "google", id: "people/c123", etag}, {kind: "drupal", id: "<uuid>"}]`.

**2. Matching.** People are matched across sources, and to rooms, by phone
number (E.164) first, then email. A bridge room's members carry the number
(the gmessages and Signal puppets do). When nothing matches, the room stays
a room, as now. A wrong match can be split again from the card, and a
missed one joined there. Those decisions are kept and win over the matcher.

**3. Google wins.** On the card, each field shows Google's value when Google
has one. Drupal's fields that Google has no place for (CRM notes, company,
anything custom) are shown below as their own section.

**4. Writes.** An edit goes to the source that owns the field: Google for
the shared fields, Drupal for CRM-only ones. Google's etag guards against
overwriting a newer change, and a conflict answers 409 with both values. A
new contact goes to Google by default; the card offers "also add to the
CRM".

## The contract (new routes)

- `GET /contacts?q=` — the list, or matches as you type.
- `GET /contacts/<id>` — the card: the merged fields, the Drupal section,
  and the rooms (sessions) that belong to it.
- `PATCH /contacts/<id>` — an edit, with the field set and the etag seen.
- `POST /contacts` — a new contact.
- `POST /contacts/<id>/text {service}` — start a chat with them. SMS goes
  through the gmessages bridge, which makes the room; the answer carries
  the new room's session.
- `/targets` Matrix rows gain `contact: {id, name}` when matched, so Messages
  can group a person's rooms without a second request.

## In the app

- **Messages, grouped by person.** One row per contact, showing their
  newest message and a small service badge for each room. Tapping it opens
  the newest room, and a switch at the top of the thread moves between their
  rooms. A Show option (and Sort By service) still offers rooms one by one.
- **The card.** The room's ⋮ (or tapping its title) opens the card: phones,
  emails, addresses and notes, each editable; the CRM section; and their
  conversations.
- **Text someone.** A ✎ on Messages opens a contact picker, then a choice
  of service, and lands in the room.
- The box and ⌕ search contacts' names and numbers too.

## Where contacts live (David, 18 Oct 2026)

- **Not somahz.com.** "somahz.com isn't the right place for it."
- **The address book and the CRM are two things** (David, 18 Oct 2026,
  agreeing a CRM per account is overkill: a Drupal and a database each,
  ~1.7 GB with updates forever, and impossible on a phone server).
  - **A personal address book for every account**, in its server's own
    store: contacts, notes and the timeline (`contacts.db`, `timeline.db`).
    This is the default and needs no Drupal.
  - **A membership CRM for an organisation**: one per organisation, not
    per account. It holds members, roles and layered access to projects,
    which is what Drupal is good at. A book may link to one if its owner
    wants (`contacts_drupal.py`, the `crm_activity` module); nothing
    depends on it.
  - So the CRM does not move to red5 (the red5-crm.sasonica.com plan is
    dropped; nothing was copied), and David's ~2,700 personal contacts are
    not seeded into it: the seed (live step 5) is for an organisation
    choosing to, not a default. Where the organisation's CRM lives, off
    somahz.com, is still open.
- **Harden the sign-in site's cookies first.** Every `<x>.sasonica.com`
  is already, or will be with the relay (`2026-10-03-off-the-tailnet.md`),
  a host its owner controls, and any of them can set a cookie for
  `.sasonica.com` that cms.sasonica.com would accept (cookie tossing:
  session fixation, a forced sign-in). So cms.sasonica.com's session and
  OAuth cookies become host-only and `__Host-`-prefixed (Secure, Path=/,
  no Domain), which a sibling cannot set or shadow, and nothing on
  sasonica.com sets a Domain=.sasonica.com cookie. To do before the relay
  hands out names or a CRM goes live on one; checked by a test that
  plants a parent-domain cookie and still signs in as the right account.
  **Done 19 Oct 2026** (websites `c63949c`). Found: Drupal's session was
  `SSESS…; domain=.cms.sasonica.com`; the front's `sas_session` and
  `sas_signin` (Path=/account) and the relay's `sas_pkce`/account cookie
  were host-only but not `__Host-`; nothing set Domain=.sasonica.com.
  Now cms.sasonica.com sends `__Host-SSESS…` (path=/, secure, no Domain;
  `sasonica_oidc`'s `HostOnlySessionConfiguration`) and the front
  `__Host-sas_session` / `__Host-sas_signin` (Path=/). Checked live: the
  anonymous `/oauth/authorize` for `sasonica-app` (PKCE) sets the new
  cookie and redirects to sign-in; a planted old-name cookie and a bogus
  new-name one are ignored (a fresh session); the front's `/signin` sets
  `__Host-sas_signin` and a cookieless callback answers `error=stale`.
  Everyone was signed out once (the names changed). Not checked: a full
  password sign-in end to end, for David to do once. **Left:** the relay
  Worker's cookies (sasonica-shell `worker/src/account.ts`, Paths
  `/join/callback` and `/account`) to `__Host-` with Path=/ — a sibling
  could still toss a `sas_account` at relay.sasonica.com/account.
- **Not inside cms.sasonica.com.** That site is the OIDC issuer for every
  account: a CRM bug there (Drupal access mistakes are common — the
  `meta.omitted` leak is one) would reach everyone's sign-in. "I'm not sure
  if sasonica.com is the right place for all of them … there are some
  security implications."
- **Sasonica holds no one's contacts.** Each server keeps its own book
  (`contacts.db`, `timeline.db`); a CRM is an optional add-on the owner
  brings. A shared Sasonica CRM for the hosted tier, if ever, is its own
  site with a space per account, kept apart from the issuer.
- **One address book per person, several per server.** A shared server
  (a household) must not mix one person's phone contacts into another's.
  Not several profiles per person: work and personal are a tag on a source
  and a filter in Messages, so one person stays one row. To do now while
  it is cheap: key the contact store and the timeline by account (`sub`),
  not by server, so a second person later is an addition, not a migration.
  **Built 18 Oct 2026** (contract §6.25 "Whose book"): per-account paths,
  not an account column — the owner's book is today's files, another
  account's the same files under `accounts/<key>/`, so nothing migrates
  and no query can forget to filter. A caller reads only its own book; an
  enrolled device that may enrol others (or `MEDIA_OWNER_ACCOUNT`) is the
  owner's. The phone and the Matrix rooms stay the owner's alone.
- **Work and personal tags**: taken by the labels (below, 19 Oct 2026) —
  Google's own labels, a filter on People; no tag system of its own.

- **Google, per account** (David, 18 Oct 2026: "let's do it but keep it in
