### How to press the button from red5

Worth writing down, because the last handover recorded that logcat, dumpsys and
adb are all closed on p8a and left the impression that nothing framework-side is
reachable. Two things are:

```sh
ssh p8a 'cmd media_session dispatch pause'          # a media key, to the addressed player
ssh p8a 'am start -n net.agentmedia.companion/.MainActivity'   # restart the app
```

`cmd input keyevent` is **not** — it needs `INJECT_EVENTS` and says so. Neither
is `dumpsys` (absent) or `cmd media_session list-sessions` (`***Error listing
sessions***`). Prefer `dispatch pause` over `play-pause` when testing: pause is
idempotent, so it proves delivery through the log without making a sound on a
phone somebody is holding.

## The app went away, and nothing said why — a fifth way to be deaf

At about 19:51 the status server stopped answering on 8770. **No crash file in
Downloads**, and David confirms he did not kill it. `am start` brought it back
clean.

The absence is itself a diagnosis — it says "not a throw" — but that is the only
thing it says, and a throw is one of seventeen ways a process ends. The crash
recorder cannot see a kill, an ANR, a low-memory reap, a freeze, or a
force-stop.

`a777483` closes that. Android will tell an app about its own corpse with no
permission at all (`getHistoricalProcessExitReasons`), and on this phone nothing
else will. Read once in `onCreate`, logged at startup, published as `last_exit`
in `/state`, five kept newest-first:

```sh
ssh p8a "curl -s 127.0.0.1:8770/state | grep -o '\"last_exit\":\[[^]]*\]'"
```

**So the actual cause of tonight's death is still unknown** — the reader was not
running when it happened. It will be answered by the next one, which is the
point. If that answer ever comes back `CRASH` with an empty Downloads directory,
the bug to chase is the recorder, not the crash.

## Two defects found by using the thing

**`call_guard` did not touch the book at all** (David's ask, `b4d0268`). Same
argument that keeps speech in the pause list and the strongest case of the
three: a book is longform speech, it runs for hours, and `sink-book`'s
`--ao=openal` ignores audio focus exactly as the other two do. The companion app
does not cover it — its focus policy ducks music and pauses speech and leaves
book alone. Verified on the device: `event-hold engaged on 2 socket(s)`.

**The auto-resume resumed things it had not paused** (`727929f`), which the book
exposed within the hour of joining the list. I paused the book at 5:12 from the
shade, triggered a mic-detect hold to test the new socket, and the release
started it playing. `resume_sockets` un-paused everything in `pause_list`
unconditionally.

Speech had hidden this for as long as it existed: the coordinator un-pauses
speech at the start of every reply anyway, so a wrong resume there is overwritten
within seconds. A book paused deliberately just plays on.

The pause path now keeps what the duck path always kept — a record of the state
it found, so it puts back that and nothing else. `held` is the twin of `ducked`.
Two things worth carrying:

- **It records on the engage pass only.** On the re-assert cycle a paused socket
  is one *we* paused a moment ago; reading that as the listener's choice would
  make every hold longer than one poll permanent. There is a test for exactly
  that, because it is the failure this fix could introduce.
- **A broker that will not answer the read is assumed to have been playing.**
  Both wrong answers cost something and they do not cost the same: resuming
  something already stopped is a keypress, stranding a paused broker is a channel
  that stays silent with nothing left that knows to start it.

Verified on p8a, book paused: `paused ... sink-book.sock (was already paused)` →
`left paused (it already was) ... sink-book.sock`.

## Long titles scroll

`591681b`, David's ask. Same semantics as `_marquee` in `cli.py` — offset derived
from how long the text has been showing, so three cards off one clock stay in
phase however often each is republished.

The restraint is the design rather than an optimisation: it runs only while a
card is **both playing and too long to fit**, and one ticker drives all three.
Notification churn is open item 3, the addressed-player slot is what the earbud
button follows, and this is the feature most able to make that worse. A paused
book therefore sits at a fixed frame — which is also the honest reading, since
nothing is happening.

**The canvas already has one** and has had for some time: `#title.scroll` in
`canvas.css`, measured after reflow in `render()`. Note `html.eink #title.scroll
{ animation: none !important }` — deliberately disabled on the Pine Note, whose
DU4 mode has no animation. So if the canvas title looks static, the question is
which device is showing it.

## A real call arrived, and proved nothing

`call_guard` engaged on a genuine call at 19:57:07 and released at 19:57:47 — the
event the last handover said had never been rehearsed. The app's `focus_events`
stayed **empty** across it.

That is *not* evidence that a call delivers no focus callback. The app held
nothing at 19:57:07: the last clip had ended at 19:53:40 with `focus: abandoned`.
You cannot be told you lost what you never took — the same lesson as item 1 this
morning, arriving from the other side. **The call test still wants a call that
overlaps something playing.** Until then the duck machinery stays where it is.
