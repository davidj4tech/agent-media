## The one thing to read first

guesswork into two log lines, and it is the reason anything below got found:


## What was learned on the device

Three things the previous handover flagged as assumptions. All three were
wrong, and each cost a sideload to find.

**Our own speech takes audio focus.** The previous handover said "our own
speech will not trigger a focus loss… worth confirming rather than assuming".
Confirmed false: `LOSS_TRANSIENT` at 18:32:20 and `GAIN` at :35, bracketing a
clip whose coordinator duck and restore land at :21 and :37. This matters — the
committed table paused music on `LOSS_TRANSIENT`, which would have stopped the
music dead for every sentence Sam speaks, while the coordinator was ducking for
the same clip. Now: **both transient losses duck; only a permanent loss
pauses.** That is David's rule (duck the music, pause the speech) applied as
the previous handover always said it should be, and it converges with
`call_guard`, which ducks phone-local music during calls rather than pausing.

**`setWillPauseWhenDucked(true)` is load-bearing.** From API 26 the framework
ducks the loser's own players and does not call the listener. Without the flag,
`LOSS_TRANSIENT_CAN_DUCK` never arrives: Android ducks our stream of zeros — a
no-op — and mpv plays straight through the navigation prompt. See the quote in
`FocusControl`'s javadoc.

**The earbuds only ever send `KEYCODE_MEDIA_PAUSE`.** Not the toggle. Five
presses, three consecutive, every one a dedicated PAUSE while the session
correctly reported `PAUSED` — so the play half never arrived and pause-then-play
left music stuck. They are not confused: the silent `AudioTrack` follows
`loaded()`, so it keeps writing zeros while mpv is paused, which keeps A2DP
flowing, which the headset reads as "audio is playing".

That belief cannot be corrected without dropping the silent track on pause, and
dropping it surrenders the addressed-player slot — the press that would win it
back is the play button we would then not receive. So `ButtonPolicy` reads the
key against mpv's state instead: PAUSE while already paused means play. The
`PLAY_PAUSE` toggle is deliberately left alone, because the framework resolves
it from the PlaybackState we publish and gets it right.

Written, tested (116 host checks), installed, and **acting** on p8a. What is

Also still unanswered from the previous handover: whether taking focus disturbs

## Known open, in the order I would take them

1. **The speech title does not appear on the car display while Sam speaks.**
   Expected: the app publishes exactly one MediaSession, for the *music*
   channel, and speech is a different mpv (`sink-speech.sock`) the app cannot
   see at all. Do **not** fix this by publishing a second session — two
   sessions compete for the addressed-player slot, which is what the spike
   learned and what the transport fix above depends on. The fix is one session
   whose metadata follows whichever channel is in front: speech while a clip
   plays, music otherwise. Which means it lands with (2).

2. **The speech bridge on `127.0.0.1:6602`.** Still the blocker for the "pause
   the speech" half, and now also for (1). Another
   `mpv-music-bridge-local`-shaped runit service into `sink-speech.sock`.

3. **Automate's mic-detect did not survive the reboot.** The external hold last
   fired 2026-08-13 14:38; p8a rebooted ~17:26 today and `call_guard` came back
   with it, Automate did not. This is the "Automate boot-start" item already on
   the previous pick-up list, not a regression from tonight.

4. **`IpcTest` is flaky.** Its two reconnect checks failed once under a
   concurrent build and passed on three consecutive re-runs. Timing, not a
   regression, but it will mislead someone eventually.

5. `restore_volume` reports `100` until the first duck — `MpvState.volume`'s
   default rather than a real reading. Harmless while `owes_unduck` is false,
   but it is the same shape as the bug `c2db694` fixed and worth removing.

## yt-dlp, which ate an hour and is worth not repeating

Music would not play: `HTTP Error 403` on the format download, extraction fine.
It reproduces with bare `yt-dlp` on the phone, which is the quickest proof it is
not our plumbing.

The cause was **not** a stale install. 2026.07.04 was the newest release on both
Termux's repo and PyPI; only the **nightly** could fetch. Being up to date and
being able to do the job had come apart.

Two dotfiles changes followed:

- **`a8c69c6` — repair at the point of failure.** `play-local`'s download now
  calls `termux-refresh-ytdlp --escalate` once on failure and retries. The real
  download is the probe: no canary video, no scheduled wake-up, and correct
  after a month of uptime. Stamped at 6h in `$CACHE/.ytdlp-repair` so a dead
  video cannot reinstall yt-dlp once per track; the stamp lives in `play-local`
  so a hand-run refresh is never silently skipped.
- **`a96c6e6` — decouple the refresh from this repo moving.**
  `termux-refresh-ytdlp` only ran from `apply`'s post-hook, and
  `dotfiles-auto-update` returns at "already up to date" long before `apply`.
  A host in sync therefore never refreshed — p8a sat six weeks that way while
  rebooting regularly. The hook now runs up front;
  `DOTFILES_TERMUX_HOOKS_DONE` stops `apply` repeating it.
