# Handover — 2026-08-15: the speech half, and four ways to be deaf

Continues `2026-08-14-night.md`, whose open item 1 is closed. That session made
the app stop guessing whose focus loss it is; this one gave it the other half of
David's rule — **duck the music, pause the speech** — and then spent most of its
length discovering that the app could not hear the interruptions the feature
exists for.

Ten commits in this repo, all pushed and all verified on p8a rather than
reasoned about: `66cf236` (the speech pause), `92f801c` (the resume window),
`8dbd9a1` (the volume goes back where it came from), `b02332a` (focus for
speech), `0552599` and `8f26e26` (the card), `9fb8ba2` (the quiet detector),
`3e9e423` (call_guard loses music), `2032a9d` (a permanent loss kills the

## The one thing to read first

**The app was deaf, four separate times, in four different ways.** Each looked
like a policy bug and none was. In the order they were found:

1. **It never took focus for speech.** Focus followed `state.loaded()` — the
   music mpv alone — so a spoken reply with no track behind it left the app
   holding nothing. You cannot be told you lost what you never took. The
   interruption that lands mid-sentence is the entire point of the speech half,
   and it is precisely the case where music is least likely to be playing.
2. **A permanent `LOSS` killed the request and the app went on believing it held
   focus.** `AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS` ends the `AudioFocusRequest`; nothing more arrives
   through it, ever. The re-request is guarded by "do we already hold it?", so
   one permanent loss ended the feature for the session. It appeared to work
   twice this morning only because both were the first loss after an install.
3. **The fix was never installed.** A whole round trip was spent arguing with
   code the phone had never run. `/state` now reports the commit it was built
   from — see below.
4. **Firefox never asks for focus at all.** Not a bug on our side and not
   fixable from here: it plays straight over Sam, committing exactly the sin mpv
   commits and this app exists to compensate for.

The pattern is worth carrying: when the phone does nothing, ask what the app was
*holding* before asking what it decided.

## What the speech half does

`SpeechPolicy`, beside `FocusPolicy`, driving the speech mpv on 6602. A focus
loss that is not our own pauses the clip; the `GAIN` resumes it. Ducking speech
is not an option — a half-heard sentence is a lost sentence and nothing replays
it — which is why it is a second class rather than a flag on the first.

Two asymmetries with music, both deliberate:

**A permanent loss still owes the resume.** mpv's `pause` belongs to the player,
not the clip: it outlives the file open when it was set. A stranded music pause
costs a button press; a stranded speech pause costs *every later reply*, and
nothing presses play on the speech broker.

**Whether the `GAIN` resumes depends on how long it was gone** — David's rule,
asked and answered:

| Pause stood | On the way back |
|---|---|
| under 2 min (`RESUME_WINDOW_MS`) | resume; the sentence picks up where it stopped |
| over it | nothing — the pause stands, David lifts it. `call_guard`'s policy for calls, kept |
| over 5 min (`RESUME_DEADLINE_MS`) | **discard** — `stop`, then clear the pause |

The last row is not a third policy but the thing neither of the others may
leave behind: a broker paused with nothing left that knows to unpause it.

## The signal Android does not send

A permanent loss is what a real media app takes — the YouTube app claimed the
output with `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN` at 08:58:19 — and **Android says nothing when it
stops.** So the resume window could never fire for the apps that matter most.

The app now listens instead: `isMusicActive()` going false for two consecutive
polls feeds the same `GAIN` branch. A heuristic where the callback is a fact, so
it is bounded — only while a pause is owed, only while our own music is idle.

The part that took a moment to see, and that the device proved load-bearing:
**the silent `AudioTrack` has to stop while listening.** Our zeros are a player
like any other and `isMusicActive()` counts them, so a silent track left running
would answer "someone is playing" forever. To hear whether anyone else is making
a noise, stop making one yourself.

It is load-bearing twice over: after a permanent loss the app deliberately does
*not* re-request focus (that would stop the video David just started, one poll
after getting out of its way), so the quiet detector is the only path back until
we next have something to play.

## The card describes one channel and drives that same channel

`PlaybackState` and every transport callback used to stay with music while the
metadata followed the front channel. What that produced: a card titled `Sam`
reporting `STOPPED`, showing a play triangle, sending `pause=false` to an idle
music mpv. Five consecutive `transport: play` at 08:22 did nothing at all.

State, play, pause and stop now follow the front channel; next, previous and
seek are withdrawn while speech is in front rather than pointed at the music
underneath. Then the same day's second bug: pausing Sam dropped the front
channel, so the card that had just paused him could not start him again —
`pause -> speech` at 08:54:17, `play -> music` at 08:54:20. The front channel
now covers a clip *held* paused, and only held: the clip sink-speech merely
parks still hands the front back to music.

## One owner per volume, one layer down

`8dbd9a1` is the fourth cause of the sentence this project keeps fixing — *the
music got quieter after Sam spoke and never came back*. The router picks a
backend by asking what is audible **now**, and liveness includes "has a track
loaded". A reply that outlasts the track it ducked flips that answer between the
two halves of one debt: the duck at 07:42:51 went to the phone, the track ended
at 07:45:01, the restore at 07:45:31 was routed to Mopidy. The router now
remembers which backend received the duck.

## call_guard has lost music

Deployed; the service restarted with `pause=['sink-speech.sock'] duck=[]`. It
does not name the music socket at all — not ducked, not paused. The app holds
focus on mpv's behalf and owns that volume now, and a second owner of one number
is the bug rather than a redundancy.

**The caveat is kept rather than hidden.** Focus ducking is proven on p8a for
another app taking the output (07:42:20, 110 → 10 → 110 on the `GAIN`) but has
**never been seen for a call**, which is by nature hard to rehearse. The duck
machinery stays in place, unused: `MEDIA_CALL_GUARD_DUCK_SOCKETS` puts it back
with no code change, and a test guards that route.

## Known open, in the order I would take them

1. **`call_guard`'s speech half**, and only after a real call has been seen to
   deliver a focus callback. It is the one interruption there is still no trace
   of, and the worst one to get wrong. Then the Automate hold flag, which may
   vanish rather than move if voice typing takes focus properly.
2. **Three channels in the shade's media player** — David asked. One card per
   active session, so three sessions: music, speech, book. Two costs: the
   addressed-player slot (my read is the music session keeps it, since the slot
   follows the silent `AudioTrack` and only music would hold one — a hypothesis
   the phone has to answer), and book needs an `mpv-book-bridge-local` beside
   the other two. The upside is more than a nicer shade: three cards would
   retire the whole front-channel mechanism, since each channel would name
   itself instead of taking turns in one slot.
3. **The silent track churns** — started and stopped every ten or twenty seconds
   across a reply's clip boundaries (09:07:00 through 09:08:21). Harmless so far,
   but it is the addressed-player slot flickering, and that slot is what the
   earbud transport depends on.
4. **The coordinator's flag is up between clips and after a reply**, which makes
   any genuine interruption arriving mid-reply read as ours: it neither ducks
   the music nor pauses the speech. Inherent to a flag that says "a response is
   in flight" rather than "which player took the output". Seen stuck true for 88 s
   with speech idle.
5. `restore_volume` still reports 100 until the first duck; `IpcTest` still
   flaky; `force-media-title` for the speech clips; red5's disk (~90%); the
   `_channel_is_playing("music")` defect in `cli.py`.

## Things that will bite

- **Check `build` in `/state` before believing a test.** `ssh p8a curl -s
  127.0.0.1:8770/state | grep -o '"build":"[^"]*"'` — it is the commit the
  running APK was built from, `+dirty` if the tree had changes. An install that
  silently does not take looks exactly like a fix that does not work, and that
  cost a round trip today.
- **The phone's clock jumped** mid-session (09:13 → 17:56 in the same log). Read
  the log's internal ordering, not its absolute times, when correlating with
  anything on red5.
- **`sink-speech.sock` is not in `$PREFIX/tmp`** with the others — it lives under
  the agent-media state dir. Drive it through the loopback bridge on 6602
  instead of guessing the path.
- **A monitor on `/log` will fire on your own replies.** Every spoken reply is a
  focus event now; filter to `focus: (LOSS|GAIN|pause|resume|nothing)` or the
  notifications feed themselves.
- Everything from last night still holds: the volume dial is David's, sideload is
  `scp` + a tap from Files, the two readouts are the whole diagnostic story,
  loopback means loopback, and the working tree is shared with sessions that
  `git reset --hard origin/main`.
