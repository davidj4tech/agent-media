# Handover — 2026-08-14 night: the app stops guessing, and three duckers become one

Continues `2026-08-14-evening.md`. That session landed audio focus and the
transport fix; this one opened the speech socket to the app, put the car display
on the right channel, and then spent most of its time on what the first duck ever
observed on the device exposed — **three separate things lowering one volume, and
losing the restore between them.**

Seven commits here, all pushed: `f3f0fb6` (metadata follows the front channel),
`5b6b280` (the bridges move into this repo), `41a462d` and `75114fc` (don't duck
for our own speech), `17c7768` (the duck debt), `3fa831d` (the media key),
`7d05977` (the in-band flag). Two in **dotfiles**: `1f762be`, `3ff3c19`.

Everything below was verified on p8a, not reasoned about.

## The one thing to read first

**The app no longer guesses whose focus loss it is — the coordinator tells it.**
`before_speech` raises `user-data/agent-media/speaking` on the speech mpv and
`after_speech` lowers it; the app observes it on the socket it already has.

That is not a refinement of a heuristic, it is a replacement for one that cannot
work. mpv takes the audio output when it **opens** a clip, and a response is
rendered and relayed well ahead of being audible. Measured on the device:

| Reply | focus loss | first audio | clip staged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20:16 | 20:16:29 | 20:16:40 (+11 s) | — |
| 20:26 | 20:26:52 | — | 20:27:29 (+37 s) |

"Is speech playing right now" answers **no** across all of that, for a loss that
is entirely ours. Any window short enough to be useful misses it; any window long
enough stops ducking the real interruptions the feature exists for. Two sessions
in a row tried to tune that window before the third stopped trying.

Do not remove the two fallbacks in `FrontChannel.ourSpeech` (speech audible, or a
clip staged within 20 s) — they cover a coordinator too old to set the flag — but
know that the flag is the one that decides in practice.

## What the duck actually looked like, and why it was wrong

The evening handover asked for the first end-to-end duck. It arrived at
**19:17:38**: `LOSS_TRANSIENT` → 130 → 10, `GAIN` at 19:17:56 → back to 130, with
music playing throughout. That also settles the other open question: **taking
focus does not disturb the pulseaudio stream mpv plays into.**

One second later the volume went back to 10 and stayed there for two hours,
because three things were ducking the same mpv:

1. **the app**, on focus loss;
2. **the coordinator**, for its own clip — it had captured our ducked 10 as the
   value to put back;
3. **`call_guard`**, still live, on calls and holds.

Each was individually correct. Together they lost the restore, and the listener
heard the sentence this project has now fixed three separate causes of: *the
music got quieter after Sam spoke and never came back* (see also `c2db694`).

Now: **one owner per volume.** The app does not duck for our own speech at all.

## The coordinator's own two bugs, both found by the same trace

**The duck was recorded in a shared row.** It lived inside
`now_playing("music")`, which other flows clear and overwrite. When that happened
between the duck and the restore, `after_speech` found no marker, returned early,
and left the phone at 10. It now has its own persisted key — `set_music_duck` /
`get_music_duck`, the twin of `set_rooms_duck` — written *before* the duck, paid
before the row is consulted, and carried across a re-duck so a second
`before_speech` cannot capture an already-ducked volume as the baseline. That
last guard is one `_rooms_duck` has always had and the music path never did.

**The fallback baseline was 45.** A Mopidy-era number on a 0-100 dial, applied to
a phone whose mpv runs `--volume=130` with a 170 ceiling. Every degraded capture
dropped the music by two thirds and left it there; it did exactly that at
20:16:45. The live backend is now asked (`nominal_volume`, same env var the
`mpv-music` service reads) and the policy constant is the last resort.

## The media key was pausing our own music

`MEDIA_ANDROID_PAUSE_HOSTS` exists to get Spotify on the phone out of the way for
speech. Since the companion app landed it cannot: our app publishes the music
session and holds the addressed-player slot, so the dispatched key reaches
**agent-media's own music**.

Which made it harmful rather than merely useless. `dumpsys media_session` is
permission-denied on non-root Termux, so the state reads `unknown`; unknown
dispatches a pause defensively and — correctly, since `dispatch play` would
*start* a session — never resumes. **Every spoken reply paused the phone's music
for good.** Seen at 20:27:13; the track was still paused thirteen minutes later.

The phone now answers this itself, in the same round trip that already classifies
playback: if the companion's status server responds on loopback, the script
reports `companion` and dispatches nothing. Verified by running the real
generated script against p8a with the pause command stubbed — it printed
`companion` and dispatched nothing.

**This is the first piece of the retirement the design predicted.** `call_guard`
and the Automate mic-detect hold flag are the same shape: workarounds for a phone
with no controllable session. Take them next, in that order.

## What the car display says now

One session, whose **metadata** follows whichever channel is in front. Speech
while a clip plays (title `Sam`, artist = the music underneath, no duration),
music otherwise. `PlaybackState`, position and every transport callback stay with
the music mpv — the framework resolves a `PLAY_PAUSE` toggle from what we report,
and answering that about a two-second clip is the class of bug `3519172` fixed.

Not a second session, for the reason the spike learned: two compete for the
addressed-player slot.

The clip's own title is unusable — sink-speech plays rendered files, so mpv's
`media-title` reads `remote-20260814T190922-18480.mp3` (checked against the
phone's speech mpv, not assumed). Putting the sentence there instead means the
speech sink setting `force-media-title` before each `loadfile`; that is a red5
change and a reasonable next step if the car display matters to David.

## Where the services live now

The two **loopback** bridges moved out of dotfiles into
`packages/core/services/`, beside the brokers, because their only consumer is the
app in this repo and the two move together. p8a wires them up as whole-dir
symlinks (`source: link` in `ansible/host_vars/p8a.yml`). They picked up this
directory's conventions on the way: a `finish` wired to `_common/crash-notify`,
and the XDG `sv-log` path rather than `$LOGDIR`, which is unset there.

The **tailnet** bridges stayed in dotfiles: which address a device exposes is a
property of the device, not of agent-media. That is the line.

| Service | Listener | Socket |
|---|---|---|
| `mpv-music-bridge-local` (this repo) | `127.0.0.1:6601` | `mpv-music.sock` |
| `mpv-speech-bridge-local` (this repo) | `127.0.0.1:6602` | `sink-speech.sock` |
| `mpv-music-bridge` (dotfiles) | tailnet `:6601` | `mpv-music.sock` |
| `mpv-speech-bridge` (dotfiles) | tailnet `:6602` | `sink-speech.sock` |

`mpv-music-bridge-local` had been created on the device by hand the night before
and never declared anywhere. Both are declared now.

## Known open, in the order I would take them

1. **Speech pausing on focus loss** — the other half of David's rule (duck the
   music, *pause the speech*). The bridge is there and the app reads it, but
   nothing writes to it. This is the last piece of the focus design.
2. **Retire `call_guard`'s music half, then the Automate hold flag.** The media
   key just showed the shape: a workaround the app makes unnecessary, still
   firing, still able to fight. Automate's mic-detect has not survived a reboot
   since 2026-08-13 anyway.
3. **`restore_volume` reports 100 until the first duck** — `MpvState.volume`'s
   default rather than a reading. Harmless while `owes_unduck` is false, carried
   over from the last handover, same shape as the bug `c2db694` fixed.
4. **`IpcTest` is flaky** — its two reconnect checks failed once under a
   concurrent build and passed on three re-runs. Timing, but it will mislead
   someone.
5. **`force-media-title` for the speech clips**, if the car display's speech line
   should carry the sentence rather than `Sam`.
6. Still parked from earlier sessions: red5's disk (~90%), and the
   `_channel_is_playing("music")` defect in `cli.py`.

## Things that will bite

- **The volume dial is David's.** He turned the phone's music to 110 tonight and
  the coordinator correctly captured and restored it — while I reset it to 130
  twice out of habit from the bug. Check `/state` and the app log before deciding
  a volume is wrong.
- **Sideload is `scp` then David opens it from Files.** `termux-open --chooser`
  does not reliably raise the installer dialog on p8a. Every device test costs an
  install and a tap; `./test/run.sh` is seconds, so prove it on the host first.
- **The two readouts are the whole diagnostic story.** `ssh p8a curl -s
  127.0.0.1:8770/state` and `/log`. `logcat` shows only Termux's uid, `dumpsys
  media_session` is denied to a non-shell uid, and adb cannot reach p8a.
- **Loopback means loopback.** 6601, 6602 and 8770 all have a tailnet or
  hand-driven twin; never widen the local ones.
- **The working tree is shared with sessions that `git reset --hard
  origin/main`.** Stage by path, push promptly.
- The phone backend expands no playlists — queue track by track with `media music
  play <uri> --where phone --add`. The offline cache under
  `~/.cache/music-offline/` is the fastest way to get known-good audio playing.
